# Component and board constraints

Research date: 2026-10-07

Scope: the supplied `BOM_4xPi-PCM_2026-10-07.csv`, the Adafruit RP2040 CAN Bus Feather (product 5724), and primary documentation from the component manufacturers. This report deliberately separates properties of the parts themselves from properties of the custom PCM PCB that a BOM cannot establish.

## Executive constraints

- The Adafruit board is marketed as an “MCP2515” Feather, but its actual CAN IC is the **MCP25625**, which combines an MCP2515-class CAN 2.0B controller and the physical transceiver. There is no separate transceiver to configure. It supports classic CAN only, up to 1 Mbit/s, not CAN FD. The board uses a **16 MHz crystal for the MCP25625** (separate from the RP2040's 12 MHz crystal), so CAN bit-timing calculations and library configuration must use 16 MHz. [Adafruit guide](https://learn.adafruit.com/adafruit-rp2040-can-bus-feather?view=all) and [Adafruit's board source](https://github.com/adafruit/Adafruit-RP2040-CAN-Bus-Feather-PCB)
- The Feather ships with a **120 ohm termination resistor enabled** through a normally closed `TERM` jumper. Only the two physical ends of the vehicle's dedicated CAN trunk should retain termination; every other node should have it cut/disconnected. [Adafruit pinout](https://learn.adafruit.com/adafruit-rp2040-can-bus-feather?view=all)
- The BOM implies **26 protected high-side outputs**: 20 one-channel BTS7004-1EPP devices and three two-channel BV2HC045EFU-C devices. It also contains four 8-channel ADS7830 ADCs (32 analog inputs), two 16-bit PCA9535 expanders (32 GPIOs), one INA219, and one TC74A0. Those counts are facts from the BOM; how they are wired is not.
- If all BOM I2C devices share one bus, the **TC74 limits the whole bus to 100 kHz**. More critically, BOM part `TC74A0-3.3VCTTR` has fixed 7-bit address **0x48**, while an ADS7830 with A1=A0=0 also uses **0x48**. The INA219 can also be strapped to 0x48. Bus partitioning or nonconflicting address straps therefore must be proven from the PCB, not guessed. [TC74 data sheet](https://ww1.microchip.com/downloads/en/devicedoc/21462c.pdf), [ADS7830 data sheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ads7830.pdf), and [INA219 data sheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ina219.pdf)
- Power-up must be fail-off at the **actual power-switch inputs**, not merely in application state. The PCA9535 resets all ports to high-impedance inputs, the BV2HC inputs have internal pull-downs, and Infineon recommends unused BTS7004 `IN` pins be pulled to ground. Whether the assembled PCB implements a safe inactive state for all 26 channels cannot be established from the BOM.
- The two high-side-switch families behave differently during faults. BTS7004 faults latch the channel off and expose analog current/fault information on `IS`; BV2HC045 is the **off-latch** member (not the auto-recovery member) and exposes two diagnostic outputs. Firmware must model the channel family and its recovery policy rather than treating all outputs as identical.

## Component facts

### Adafruit RP2040 CAN Bus Feather and MCP25625

Board-level facts from Adafruit:

- The RP2040 runs 3.3 V logic and has 264 KiB RAM and 8 MiB external QSPI flash; it has no EEPROM. Application configuration therefore needs an explicit flash persistence strategy if settings must survive power loss. [Adafruit product page](https://www.adafruit.com/product/5724)
- The MCP25625 uses the exposed SPI bus: SCK/GPIO14, MOSI/GPIO15, and MISO/GPIO8. Dedicated internal control nets are GPIO16 `CAN_STANDBY`, GPIO17 `CAN_TX0_RTS`, GPIO18 `CAN_RESET`, GPIO19 `CAN_CS`, GPIO22 `CAN_INTERRUPT`, and GPIO23 `CAN_RX0_BF`. External hardware using the exposed SPI pins must coexist with this device and use independent chip selects. [Adafruit pinout](https://learn.adafruit.com/adafruit-rp2040-can-bus-feather?view=all)
- Adafruit's schematic has a 10 kohm pull-up on CAN chip select, a 10 kohm pull-up on CAN reset, and a 10 kohm pull-down on CAN standby; the MCP25625 is clocked by a 16 MHz crystal. The board generates the transceiver's 5 V supply from 3.3 V with an AP3602 charge pump and includes CAN-line ESD protection. [Adafruit board source](https://github.com/adafruit/Adafruit-RP2040-CAN-Bus-Feather-PCB)
- The CAN terminal block is CANL, common ground, CANH. The common reference should be treated as part of the vehicle network wiring design, not omitted merely because CAN is differential. [Adafruit pinout](https://learn.adafruit.com/adafruit-rp2040-can-bus-feather?view=all)

Controller/transceiver facts from Microchip:

- The MCP25625 implements CAN 2.0B with standard and extended identifiers at up to 1 Mbit/s. It has three transmit buffers, two receive buffers, six acceptance filters, two masks, an active-low interrupt output, and SPI modes 0,0 and 1,1 at up to 10 MHz. [MCP25625 data sheet, general features](https://ww1.microchip.com/downloads/aemDocuments/documents/OTH/ProductDocuments/DataSheets/MCP25625-CAN-Controller-Data-Sheet-20005282C.pdf)
- The controller powers up or resets into **Configuration mode**. Bit timing and filters can only be changed there. Mode transitions are not complete until pending transmissions finish, so firmware must request a mode and verify `OPMOD`, not assume the write took effect. [MCP25625 data sheet, section 2](https://ww1.microchip.com/downloads/aemDocuments/documents/OTH/ProductDocuments/DataSheets/MCP25625-CAN-Controller-Data-Sheet-20005282C.pdf)
- Microchip requires a hardware reset or SPI reset after power-up to guarantee default register state. Hardware reset must remain asserted for at least 2 microseconds after VDD reaches its operating voltage; an oscillator startup timer then holds reset for 128 oscillator cycles. [MCP25625 data sheet, sections 3.13–3.14](https://ww1.microchip.com/downloads/aemDocuments/documents/OTH/ProductDocuments/DataSheets/MCP25625-CAN-Controller-Data-Sheet-20005282C.pdf)
- Normal transceiver mode requires `STBY` low. Standby mode (`STBY` high) disables the transmitter and high-speed receiver but keeps a filtered low-power receiver active. Waking from controller sleep loses the frame that caused wake-up and any frames received during the 128-cycle oscillator startup; it wakes in listen-only mode and firmware must restore both controller and transceiver normal modes. [MCP25625 data sheet, sections 2.2 and 2.5](https://ww1.microchip.com/downloads/aemDocuments/documents/OTH/ProductDocuments/DataSheets/MCP25625-CAN-Controller-Data-Sheet-20005282C.pdf)
- The transceiver specifies a 5–40 microsecond standby-to-normal delay and a 0.5–4 microsecond standby wake filter. A permanent dominant condition times out at 1.25 ms typical; an extended low on TXD disables the bus drivers until TXD returns high. [MCP25625 data sheet, table 7-8](https://ww1.microchip.com/downloads/aemDocuments/documents/OTH/ProductDocuments/DataSheets/MCP25625-CAN-Controller-Data-Sheet-20005282C.pdf)
- Diagnostics available to firmware include transmit and receive error counters; warning at 96; error-passive at 128; bus-off at transmit count 255; receive-buffer overflow flags; message-error, wake, transmit, receive, and error interrupts. The chip automatically recovers from bus-off after 128 occurrences of 11 recessive bits unless firmware intervenes. Product policy must decide whether automatic re-entry is acceptable for power-control nodes. [MCP25625 data sheet, sections 3.10–3.12](https://ww1.microchip.com/downloads/aemDocuments/documents/OTH/ProductDocuments/DataSheets/MCP25625-CAN-Controller-Data-Sheet-20005282C.pdf)
- The transceiver disconnects CANH/CANL when unpowered or browned out, protects against bus shorts and automotive transients, and thermally disables only the output drivers at about 175 degrees C nominal. These protections preserve the device; they do not prove that application messaging or outputs are safe during a fault. [MCP25625 data sheet, sections 6.3–6.5](https://ww1.microchip.com/downloads/aemDocuments/documents/OTH/ProductDocuments/DataSheets/MCP25625-CAN-Controller-Data-Sheet-20005282C.pdf)

Firmware consequence: initialize in the order reset → verify Configuration mode → program timing/filters/interrupts → clear diagnostic state → explicitly drive `CAN_STANDBY` low → request and verify Normal mode. Record and expose error counters, overflows, resets, bus-off entries, and recoveries. The 16 MHz oscillator and chosen vehicle bitrate/sample point belong in one versioned board profile.

### Infineon BTS7004-1EPP (20 one-channel outputs)

- This is a one-channel automotive smart high-side switch intended for 12 V systems. Normal operation is specified at 6–18 V, with an extended low range down to 3.1 V subject to parameter deviations. Absolute maximum supply is 28 V and suppressed load dump is specified to 35 V under Infineon's stated test conditions. Typical on-resistance is 4.4 milliohms at 25 degrees C and maximum is 8 milliohms at 150 degrees C; nominal load current is 15 A at 85 degrees C. [BTS7004-1EPP data sheet, sections 4 and 7](https://www.infineon.com/assets/row/public/documents/10/49/infineon-bts7004-1epp-datasheet-en.pdf?fileId=5546d4626102d35a016147550a725555)
- `IN` is compatible with 3.3 V and 5 V microcontrollers. Infineon says an unused `IN` must be pulled to module/device ground through 4.7 kohm. `DEN` enables diagnosis and also participates in latch reset; neither pin is a serial interface. [BTS7004-1EPP data sheet, section 5](https://www.infineon.com/assets/row/public/documents/10/49/infineon-bts7004-1epp-datasheet-en.pdf?fileId=5546d4626102d35a016147550a725555)
- With `DEN` low, `IS` is high impedance. With diagnosis enabled, `IS` sources an analog load-proportional sense current (typical current-sense ratio 20,000 at nominal current), a distinct fault current, or an open-load-in-off current. Infineon requires an external sense resistor greater than 820 ohms (400 ohms with central reverse-battery protection) and gives 1.2 kohm as typical. The actual resistor and ADC full-scale determine usable current range and thresholds. [BTS7004-1EPP data sheet, section 9](https://www.infineon.com/assets/row/public/documents/10/49/infineon-bts7004-1epp-datasheet-en.pdf?fileId=5546d4626102d35a016147550a725555)
- Protection covers overload/short-to-ground and overtemperature. Either fault switches the channel off and sets an internal latch. Reset requires either `IN` low longer than the latch-reset delay or a sufficiently long `DEN` pulse while `IN` is low, and restart is allowed only after the underlying thermal/current reactivation conditions are satisfied. Software must not repeatedly hammer a faulted load. [BTS7004-1EPP data sheet, section 8.3](https://www.infineon.com/assets/row/public/documents/10/49/infineon-bts7004-1epp-datasheet-en.pdf?fileId=5546d4626102d35a016147550a725555)
- Typical overload detection is about 117.5 A at 25 degrees C and falls with temperature; this is a short-circuit protection threshold, **not** the allowed continuous channel/load/wiring current. The PCB copper, connector, fuse, ambient temperature, and enclosure cooling set the practical limit. [BTS7004-1EPP data sheet, section 8](https://www.infineon.com/assets/row/public/documents/10/49/infineon-bts7004-1epp-datasheet-en.pdf?fileId=5546d4626102d35a016147550a725555)
- At 13.5 V the specified maximum switch-on time is 210 microseconds and switch-off time is 220 microseconds. Diagnosis cannot be considered valid immediately; Infineon bounds the overall diagnosis settling in relation to output turn-on plus `IS` settling. Programmable flash-pattern resolution should therefore stay comfortably above device switching/diagnostic settling, and diagnostic samples need explicit blanking. [BTS7004-1EPP data sheet, sections 7.4 and 9.4](https://www.infineon.com/assets/row/public/documents/10/49/infineon-bts7004-1epp-datasheet-en.pdf?fileId=5546d4626102d35a016147550a725555)
- Switching inductive loads invokes the device's active clamp and dissipates energy in the switch. “Protected” does not mean unlimited solenoid/compressor-clutch energy; each load's inductance/current and repetitive switching duty must be validated thermally. All OUT pins must be tied together and PCB traces sized for maximum possible current. [BTS7004-1EPP data sheet, sections 3.2 and 7.2.2](https://www.infineon.com/assets/row/public/documents/10/49/infineon-bts7004-1epp-datasheet-en.pdf?fileId=5546d4626102d35a016147550a725555)

Firmware consequence: treat each channel as `off`, `on`, `fault-latched`, or `recovery-pending`; distinguish open-load, proportional-current, and fault ranges; apply a post-switch diagnostic blanking interval; rate-limit retries; persist fault counters. Exact thresholds require the board's `RSENSE`, analog divider/reference, load rating, and thermal characterization.

### ROHM BV2HC045EFU-C (three devices, six outputs)

- This is an AEC-Q100 dual high-side switch with a 6–19 V rated supply range, 41 V protected output rating, 45 milliohm typical on-resistance, a fixed overcurrent threshold specified as 21 A minimum, and an externally adjustable steady-state threshold in the 1–10 A range. The `C` variant uses **off-latch** thermal shutdown; the `D` sibling is the auto-recovery version. [ROHM product announcement](https://www.rohm.com/news-detail?defaultGroupId=false&news-title=industry-s-first-intelligent-power-devices-enabling-standalone-system-protection)
- Pins are VBB, SET, DLY, GND, IN1, ST1, ST2, IN2, four OUT2 pins, four OUT1 pins, and a VBB exposed pad. IN1/IN2 have internal pull-downs. SET selects the variable overcurrent threshold through an external resistor; DLY selects the inrush/overcurrent mask time through an external capacitor. ST1/ST2 are diagnostic outputs. [ROHM SPICE modeling report](https://fscdn.rohm.com/en/products/databook/applinote/ic/power/ipd/bv2hc045efu-c_spice_an-e.pdf)
- ROHM's manufacturer model uses 70 microseconds on and 50 microseconds off propagation delay, 100/50 microseconds diagnostic assertion/deassertion, and 0.3 V/microsecond output slew as design values under its specified 14 V/6.5 ohm example. The report explicitly says simulated and sample values are guidance rather than guaranteed limits and must be verified on the actual PCB. [ROHM SPICE modeling report](https://fscdn.rohm.com/en/products/databook/applinote/ic/power/ipd/bv2hc045efu-c_spice_an-e.pdf)
- Protection includes thermal shutdown, overcurrent detection, undervoltage lockout, active clamp, open-load detection, and dual error flags. The fixed high threshold handles inrush while the RSET/CDLY network defines lower sustained-current behavior and its mask time. [ROHM selection guide](https://fscdn.rohm.com/en/products/databook/catalog/common/P_General-purpse_ICs_EN.pdf)

Firmware consequence: these six channels require a different driver and diagnostic decoder from BTS7004 channels. The actual `RSET` and `CDLY` values are safety-critical configuration facts and must be recovered from schematic/measurement before load limits or retry behavior can be specified. Do not infer an auto-retry sequence from the BV2HD sibling: the BOM is explicitly BV2HC, the off-latch part.

### TI PCA9535DWR (two devices, 32 GPIOs)

- Each device provides 16 I/Os, two 8-bit input registers, two output-latch registers, polarity inversion, direction control, one open-drain active-low `INT`, three address pins (0x20–0x27), and I2C up to 400 kHz. I/O supply range is 2.3–5.5 V and port inputs are 5 V tolerant. [PCA9535 data sheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/pca9535.pdf)
- At power-on, all 16 I/Os are high-impedance **inputs**, output latches contain ones, and polarity inversion is disabled. Changing direction to output without first programming the output latch can therefore drive high. Safe initialization is: write the desired inactive output-latch values first, then change only the intended pins to outputs. [PCA9535 data sheet, register map](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/pca9535.pdf)
- There is no reset pin or software-reset command. A full reset requires cycling VCC below 0.2 V (or satisfying the documented POR profile). Firmware should verify register readback rather than assume a processor reset also reset the expander. [PCA9535 data sheet, sections 8.2.1 and 10.1](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/pca9535.pdf)
- Any input edge asserts `INT`. Reading the affected 8-bit port or the input returning to its previous state clears it; reading the other port does not. Edges occurring during the read ACK/NACK clock can be lost or produce a short interrupt. This is an edge notification, not a queued event log, so switch inputs still need debouncing and a complete port snapshot. [PCA9535 data sheet, section 8.2.3](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/pca9535.pdf)
- TI documents an interrupt erratum: if the last command pointer remains 0x00 and another I2C slave acknowledges a read address, `INT` can deassert incorrectly. The workaround is to write a command pointer other than 0x00 after reading PCA9535 port 0 or before reading another slave. [PCA9535 data sheet, section 8.2.3.1](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/pca9535.pdf)

Firmware consequence: use shadow registers and whole-byte writes, stage inactive values before enabling outputs, read both ports on interrupt, debounce in software, implement the documented command-pointer workaround, and periodically read back configuration. Whether these expanders drive power-switch inputs, read status lines, or serve connector I/O is a PCB fact still missing.

### TI ADS7830IPWR (four devices, 32 ADC inputs)

- Each device is an 8-bit SAR ADC with eight single-ended or four differential muxed channels, a built-in 2.5 V reference/buffer, 5 microsecond conversion time, and up to 70 ksamples/s at 3.4 MHz I2C. At 400 kHz the specified maximum throughput is 10 ksamples/s; at 100 kHz it is 2.5 ksamples/s. Supply is 2.7–3.6 V for the low-voltage specification or 4.75–5.25 V for the 5 V specification. [ADS7830 data sheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ads7830.pdf)
- Hardware address is 0x48–0x4B from A1/A0, sampled at power-up. The command byte chooses single-ended/differential mode, mux channel, internal-reference state, and ADC power state. There is no persistent configuration register: every conversion request must carry the intended mode. [ADS7830 data sheet, pages 13–14](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ads7830.pdf)
- The internal reference defaults off. If firmware turns it on after it was off, it must wait for reference settling before expecting 8-bit accuracy and must keep the command's PD1 bit set for subsequent samples. Alternatively, the PCB may supply an external reference; that is not knowable from the BOM. [ADS7830 data sheet, internal-reference sequence](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ads7830.pdf)
- Full-scale input is the selected reference; at 2.5 V one LSB is about 9.8 mV. The analog pin absolute operating range and any external divider/filter determine what vehicle voltage or BTS sense current is measurable without damage or saturation. [ADS7830 data sheet, electrical characteristics](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ads7830.pdf)

Firmware consequence: use explicit per-channel descriptors for ADC address, mux code, reference mode, scaling, and fault thresholds; do not assume the physical CH number equals the output-channel number. If the shared bus is held to 100 kHz by TC74, four ADCs cannot each sustain their headline rate; total bus schedule and diagnostic latency must be budgeted.

### TI INA219AIDR

- The BOM suffix is the A-grade SOIC-8 device. It measures bidirectional shunt voltage with programmable ranges of +/-40, 80, 160, or 320 mV and bus voltage with 16 or 32 V full-scale register scaling, but the physical IN+/IN- pins must remain within the 0–26 V operating range. Supply is 3–5.5 V. [INA219 data sheet](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ina219.pdf)
- It supports 16 addresses from 0x40–0x4F by strapping A0/A1 to GND, VS, SDA, or SCL. The pins are sampled at each communication, so their wiring must be stable before bus traffic. [INA219 data sheet, table 1](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ina219.pdf)
- Power-up/reset defaults are 32 V register range, +/-320 mV shunt range, 12-bit continuous shunt-plus-bus conversion. The calibration register defaults to zero, so Current and Power registers remain zero until firmware calculates and writes calibration from the **actual shunt resistance**. Configuration and calibration are volatile and must be restored after every power-up/reset. [INA219 data sheet, default settings and register map](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ina219.pdf)
- A 12-bit conversion takes 532 microseconds typical, 586 microseconds maximum, per bus or shunt conversion. Averaging can extend one channel to 68.1 ms; a continuous shunt-plus-bus cycle includes both configured conversions. Firmware should use the conversion-ready bit rather than stale-data timing guesses. Recovery from power-down requires 40 microseconds. [INA219 data sheet, ADC settings](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ina219.pdf)
- The I2C/SMBus interface resets if either line stays low more than 28 ms. Register contents update 4 microseconds after a write; above 1 MHz SCL, a same-pointer read requires that delay. [INA219 data sheet, bus and register timing](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ina219.pdf)
- TI warns that shorts and inductive kickback can exceed the 26 V common-mode/differential rating, and that excessive input dV/dt can cause overstress; its guidance includes input filtering and, for the described dV/dt case, 10 ohm series resistors. This matters if the chip is exposed directly to an automotive battery rail. [INA219 data sheet, section 8.4.1](https://www.ti.com/lit/ds/symlink/ina219.pdf)

Firmware consequence: no current/power value is trustworthy until the shunt value, address, bus location, and calibration are established. If BOM `R1` (300 micro-ohms) is the shunt, that still needs schematic/measurement confirmation and system-level calibration; at that value, layout copper and Kelvin sensing become a substantial part of the error budget.

### Microchip TC74A0-3.3VCTTR

- This exact suffix is optimized for a 3.3 V supply and fixed at 7-bit I2C address **0x48**. It reports signed 8-bit temperature with 1 degree C resolution, +/-2 degrees C maximum accuracy from 25–85 degrees C and +/-3 degrees C from 0–125 degrees C. Supply range is 2.7–5.5 V, but accuracy degrades about 1 degree C per volt away from the nominal characterized supply. [TC74 data sheet](https://ww1.microchip.com/downloads/en/devicedoc/21462c.pdf)
- Conversion rate is 4 samples/s minimum, 8 samples/s typical. After power-on, data-ready can take as long as 250 ms. The serial clock is limited to 100 kHz. [TC74 data sheet, electrical characteristics](https://ww1.microchip.com/downloads/en/devicedoc/21462c.pdf)
- The SHDN bit stops conversion and freezes the temperature register while I2C remains operational. Standby is not a fresh reading. Output loading and nearby heat sources cause self-heating error, so the sensor's physical position matters. [TC74 data sheet, section 3.1](https://ww1.microchip.com/downloads/en/devicedoc/21462c.pdf)

Firmware consequence: wait for data-ready after startup/wake, reject stale samples, poll no faster than the conversion rate, and treat this as board temperature—not necessarily switch junction, ambient air, or enclosure temperature—until placement is documented.

## PCB-connectivity unknowns

None of the following can be answered reliably from the BOM. They require the custom PCB schematic/netlist, board files, continuity measurements, or a traced pin map.

1. **Feather-to-PCM interface:** which Feather header pins carry I2C, output enable, interrupt, board identity, watchdog/fail-safe, or any other custom signals; how the PCM powers the Feather; and whether Feather reset/power-enable is controlled externally.
2. **I2C topology and addresses:** whether all seven I2C ICs share one bus; PCA9535 A2:A0 straps; ADS7830 A1:A0 straps; INA219 A1:A0 straps; whether the TC74/ADS7830 0x48 collision exists; pull-up values/rail; bus capacitance; and whether any level shifting or bus isolation is present.
3. **Power-output map:** which PMIC channel reaches which fuse holder/terminal/output; which 20 positions use BTS7004 versus which six use BV2HC045; connector pinout; copper width/weight; fuse rating; and per-channel continuous/inrush/inductive load rating.
4. **Fail-safe polarity:** what drives every BTS `IN`/`DEN`, BV `IN1/IN2`, and any global enable; external pull states; what happens while the RP2040 and PCA9535 pins are inputs; and whether an I2C expander failure can leave an output on.
5. **Diagnostics map:** which BTS `IS` nodes feed which ADS7830 channels; sense-resistor values; BV ST1/ST2 routing and pull-ups; whether diagnostic enables are per-channel or shared; analog filter/divider constants; and whether outputs can be read back electrically.
6. **BV2HC protection programming:** exact RSET and CDLY per device, their tolerance, and whether both channels on a device share the resulting threshold/mask as the IC interface implies.
7. **INA219 measurement path:** whether `R1` is its shunt, shunt polarity, Kelvin routing, monitored rail, bus voltage at the IC pins, input filtering/transient protection, and intended current range/calibration.
8. **TC74 placement:** physical location relative to high-current switches and copper, which determines what temperature it actually represents.
9. **Identity selection:** whether either PCM already has a strap, jumper, connector coding, or spare GPIO suitable for engine-bay/rear identity; its electrical default; and whether it remains readable after other buses initialize.
10. **CAN installation:** which physical nodes are at the two ends, which termination jumpers are intact, stub lengths, cable type, ground/reference routing, connector pinout, shield practice, and chosen bitrate. “Both PCM boards have terminators” is correct only if they are the physical endpoints.
11. **Automotive power front end:** reverse-battery, jump-start, load-dump, surge, fuse, grounding, and regulator behavior ahead of both the Feather and power switches. Individual IC protection ratings do not establish module-level compliance or survival.

## Information to capture next

The next hardware-documentation pass should produce, in this order:

1. High-resolution front/back PCB photos and PCB revision/serial markings.
2. A Feather header pin table and power-tree diagram.
3. An I2C inventory measured with an address scan, with each device isolated or correlated to strap continuity so collisions are not masked.
4. A 26-row channel table: connector/fuse position, switch IC/channel, control source/pin, diagnostic source/ADC pin, sense/divider values, fuse, intended load, and measured off/on behavior.
5. Continuity-confirmed RSET/CDLY, INA219 shunt, board-ID straps, CAN termination, and all reset/pull resistors.
6. Bench tests using current-limited supplies and dummy loads before vehicle loads: boot/reset fail-off, stuck-I2C recovery, CAN loss/bus-off behavior, open-load and short diagnostics, latch/reset behavior, PWM/flash timing, and thermal derating.

Until that map exists, firmware should represent every board-specific fact as an explicit, reviewable board profile and start with all power outputs disabled. No channel should be enabled merely because an I2C device acknowledged or a guessed address/pin map matched old code.
